For physicians

Measure what happens between appointments.
Decide from data, not recall.

TrackDX is a symptom tracker that runs the statistics you and the patient need to manage their condition. Patients quickly log episodes, triggers, and medications. TrackDX leverages advanced statistical models and exports a Physician Summary for the visit — frequency over time, each medication's measured effect, efficacy of treatments, and trigger odds ratios. So you get data-driven insights instead of a shrug from the patient when you ask them how they are doing.

You get effects, not impressions.

A typical visit leaves about fifteen minutes to work out what changed since the last one. The Physician Summary puts that answer on paper before the conversation starts.

💊

Each medication scored separately

Odds of an episode while on it, and its effect on severity — both adjusted for the patient's triggers. When a patient is on three preventatives, you can see which one is doing the work.

⚡

Triggers ranked and mutually adjusted

Adjusted odds let you see which trigger is most harmful — not just which one the patient noticed most recently.

📈

Procedures measured before and after

Pre and post severity, overall and by symptom, with how long the improvement lasted. You know whether it's working — and which symptoms it's helping most.

🗓

Frequency and severity over time

Episodes per 90-day period, the last 90 days against the 90 before, and a severity trend with each procedure marked on it.

It fits the condition

Prior authorizations, with the evidence already gathered

Payers often want the same things before they approve a newer medication, a repeat injection, or surgery: how often symptoms occur, how severe they are, which treatments were tried, for how long, and whether they worked. Step-therapy rules ask for documented trial and failure of the cheaper options first. Reconstructing that from memory at the visit is slow and easy to dispute. A patient who logs in TrackDX arrives with it already measured.

Treatment history, dated

Every preventative with the days the patient was on it, current medications with start dates, and procedures with their dates — the treatment course in one place.

Response measured, not recalled

Each medication's effect on episode odds and severity while the patient was on it — so a medication that didn't help is documented as not helping, over a stated number of days.

Frequency over a defined period

Episodes per 90-day period and the last 90 days against the previous 90 for episodic conditions; for daily ones, the count of mild, moderate and severe days and how many days were logged.

Whether relief lasted

For each procedure: average severity before and after, the change for every symptom, and how many logged days stayed at least 20% below the pre-procedure average — useful when a payer asks whether a prior injection gave lasting relief.

Criteria differ between payers and plans. The Physician Summary is patient-reported data to support your clinical documentation, not a replacement for it.

Nothing to set up on your side

No portal to log into, no integration, no account. Because TrackDX developers never receive patients’ health data, there is no data-sharing agreement to set up — the patient chooses what to hand you. The patient generates the summary on their phone in seconds and hands it to you — as a PDF, printed, or on the screen.

Private by design

Everything the patient logs stays on their device — nothing is uploaded to a server, sold, or shared. The summary reaches you only because they chose to hand it over.

Try it with your patients

Three steps, one appointment cycle.

  1. Point them to trackdx.net or either app store. TrackDX is on the App Store and Google Play.
  2. Ask them to log for 30 days. The first 30 days are free with everything unlocked. After that, a one-time $1.99 — no subscription and no account.
  3. Have them bring the summary to the next visit. Patients with existing records can import them from a spreadsheet, so they don't start from zero.
